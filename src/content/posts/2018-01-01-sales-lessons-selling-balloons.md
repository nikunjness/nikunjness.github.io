On New Year's evening, a friend of mine decided to celebrate differently. He invited a few of us to help balloon sellers at traffic signals sell their balloons.

I found the idea interesting and joined in. It turned out to be a surprisingly good lesson in selling.

## 1. Personalize your pitch

Changing the language, the greeting, and the story for each car helped us sell more. Figure out who your customer is, understand their culture, find a point of connection, and use it in your pitch.

## 2. Use empathy

Telling people about the impact they were making, and how kind it was of them to buy, made a real difference. People like to feel good about a purchase, not just make it.

## 3. Smile

Many people were reluctant to even roll down their windows. A request made with a genuine smile removed a lot of that reluctance. The experience of being sold to matters as much as the product.

## 4. Create the need and show the bigger picture

People rarely buy the thing itself. Help them see the bigger picture of what the purchase means, and the need becomes clear.

## 5. You can't close every deal

Some people will say no, and that's fine. Leave them with a pleasant experience and thank them for their time. You never know who remembers that.

## 6. Peer pressure is real

When people see others buying, saying yes becomes easier. Social proof works at a traffic signal just like it works on a landing page.

---

I've sold software, services, and ideas to companies of every size since then. These six lessons still hold.
