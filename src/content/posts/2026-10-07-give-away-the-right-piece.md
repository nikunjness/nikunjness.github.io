A $5 Chrome extension put Snezzi inside 60 browsers in 30 days.

97% of those installs came from the US. We're building from India.

That's a small number. But it taught me something about distribution that I think a lot of early-stage founders, including past versions of me, get wrong.

## What we built

The extension audits any webpage for technical SEO and AI visibility, right inside the browser. It's free and doesn't need a signup.

A lot of SEO advice treats Chrome extensions as a backlink tactic: publish one, get a link from the Chrome Web Store, move on. The backlink is useful. But that framing misses the bigger opportunity.

## Who installs it matters more than how many

Someone installing our extension isn't casually reading an article. They're actively auditing a webpage for the exact problems Snezzi helps companies solve.

So the extension lets them experience a small part of our expertise before they visit our website, book a call, or talk to anyone on our team. By the time they reach us, they've already seen how we think about the problem.

Sixty organic installs from the market we want to reach is a much stronger signal than the link. It means the extension is doing more than pointing at Snezzi. It's doing a small piece of the selling.

## The hard part moved

Because AI has made software so much cheaper to build, building the extension wasn't the difficult part.

The difficult part was choosing which piece of our product to give away.

That choice is harder than it sounds. Give away too little and nobody cares. Give away the wrong thing and you attract people who will never buy. Give away too much and there's no reason to talk to you.

For us, the answer was the ability to audit any webpage for SEO and AI visibility, directly in the browser. It's genuinely useful on its own, it's used at exactly the moment someone is thinking about the problem we solve, and it naturally raises the next question: "OK, so how do I fix this?"

## A pattern worth stealing

If you're an early-stage founder, especially one building from outside the market you sell to, here's the version of this I'd try:

1. **Find the moment your buyer is already working on the problem.** Not reading about it. Doing it.
2. **Give them one real piece of your product for that moment.** Free, fast, no signup.
3. **Make it show how you think,** not just what you do.
4. **Measure who installs it, not just how many.** A small number from the right market beats a big number from the wrong one.

When building gets cheap, judgment about what to build, and what to give away, becomes the advantage.
